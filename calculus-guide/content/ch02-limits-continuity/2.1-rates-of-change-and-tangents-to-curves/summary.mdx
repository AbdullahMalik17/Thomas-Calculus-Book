# Section 2.1: Rates of Change and Tangents to Curves

Calculus originated from two fundamental geometric and physical questions:
1. **The Tangent Problem**: How do we define and calculate the slope of a curve at a single point?
2. **The Velocity Problem**: How do we determine the exact speed and direction of a moving object at a single instant in time?

Both problems lead inexorably to the concept of a **limit of a difference quotient**.

---

## 1. Average Rates of Change and Secant Lines

Given a function $y = f(x)$, the change in $x$ from $x_1$ to $x_2$ is $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$, and the corresponding change in $y$ is $\Delta y = f(x_2) - f(x_1)$.

The **average rate of change** of $f$ over $[x_1, x_2]$ is:

$$
\text{Average Rate of Change} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

Letting $x_1 = x_0$ and $x_2 = x_0 + h$ where $h \neq 0$:

$$
\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}
$$

Geometrically, this ratio represents the slope of the **secant line** through points $P(x_0, f(x_0))$ and $Q(x_0 + h, f(x_0 + h))$.

---

## 2. Instantaneous Rate of Change and the Tangent Line

To define the rate of change at the single point $x_0$, we let the increment $h$ become arbitrarily small, bringing $Q$ closer and closer to $P$.

The **slope of the curve** (or instantaneous rate of change) at $P(x_0, f(x_0))$ is:

$$
m = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}
$$

If this limit exists, the **tangent line** to $y = f(x)$ at $(x_0, f(x_0))$ is the line passing through $P$ with slope $m$:

$$
y - f(x_0) = m(x - x_0)
$$

---

## 3. Physical Application: Velocity and Motion

If $s(t)$ represents the position of an object moving along a coordinate axis at time $t$:
- **Average Velocity** over $[t, t + \Delta t]$:
  $$
  v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{s(t + \Delta t) - s(t)}{\Delta t}
  $$
- **Instantaneous Velocity** at time $t$:
  $$
  v(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{s(t + \Delta t) - s(t)}{\Delta t}
  $$

---

## 4. Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing Average and Instantaneous Rates**: An average rate requires an interval $[a, b]$, whereas an instantaneous rate is evaluated at a single point via a limit.
- **Evaluating at $h = 0$ Directly**: Setting $h = 0$ prematurely yields the indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$. The difference quotient must always be algebraically simplified before taking the limit.
