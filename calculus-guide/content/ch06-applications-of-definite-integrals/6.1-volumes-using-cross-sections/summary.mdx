# Section 6.1: Volumes Using Cross-Sections

Calculating volumes of three-dimensional solids by slicing them into thin slabs with known geometric cross-sections.

---

## 1. General Slicing Method

If cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis have area $A(x)$:

$$
V = \int_a^b A(x) dx
$$

Common cross-sectional geometries:
- **Square of side $s$**: $A = s^2$
- **Equilateral triangle of side $s$**: $A = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} s^2$
- **Semicircle of diameter $s$**: $A = \frac{\pi}{8} s^2$

---

## 2. Solids of Revolution: Disks and Washers

- **Rotation around $x$-axis**:
  $$
  V = \pi \int_a^b \left( [R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2 \right) dx
  $$
- **Rotation around $y$-axis**:
  $$
  V = \pi \int_c^d \left( [R(y)]^2 - [r(y)]^2 \right) dy
  $$
