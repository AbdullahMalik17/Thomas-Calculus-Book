Chapter 1 · Sections 1.1 – 1.2 · Core Foundations

Natural Domains, Vertical Line Test & Parity Symmetry

  • Natural Domain Trap: Exclude division by zero ($g(x)=0$), negative even roots ($u(x) < 0$), and non-positive logarithms ($u(x) \le 0$). Note: $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, never $\pm x$.
  • Even Symmetry ($y$-axis mirror): $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in D$ (e.g. $x^2, \cos x, |x|$).
  • Odd Symmetry ($180^\circ$ origin spin): $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in D$ (e.g. $x^3, \sin x, \tan x$). Note: $f(x) = x^3 + 1$ is neither!
$$f(-x) = f(x) \iff \text{Even}, \qquad f(-x) = -f(x) \iff \text{Odd}$$

Chapter 1 · Sections 1.2 – 1.3 · Algebraic & Trig Mastery

Composite Domains, Graph Shifts & Radian Calculus

1. Composite Domain Law

$(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$

Never inspect only the simplified formula! Input $x$ must satisfy both $x \in \text{Dom}(g)$ and $g(x) \in \text{Dom}(f)$.

$$f(u)=u^2,\; g(x)=\sqrt{x} \implies x \ge 0$$
2. Shifting & Scaling

Inside $x$ = Opposite

$y = f(x - h) + k$ shifts right by $h$ and up by $k$. $y = f(cx)$ compresses horizontally by factor $c$.

$$y = a\,f(b(x - h)) + k$$
3. Radian Requirement

Why Radians Are Mandatory

All calculus derivative formulas ($\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$) require $x$ in radians ($s = r\theta$). In degrees, an extra $\frac{\pi}{180}$ factor appears!

$$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$$

Chapter 2 · Sections 2.2 & 2.4 · Analytical Limits

Resolving $0/0$ Indeterminate Forms & The Squeeze Theorem

  • Punctured Neighborhood Principle: $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ depends ONLY on values near $c$, never on $f(c)$ itself.
  • Three $0/0$ Weapons: Factoring & canceling, multiplying by radical conjugates ($\sqrt{a}\pm\sqrt{b}$), or using the fundamental trig limit.
  • Sandwich (Squeeze) Theorem: If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ near $c$ and $\lim_{x\to c} g(x) = \lim_{x\to c} h(x) = L$, then $\lim_{x\to c} f(x) = L$.
$$\lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1, \qquad \lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{\theta} = 0$$

Chapter 2 · Section 2.3 · Interactive Simulation

The Precise $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ Definition of a Limit

For every target tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a radius $\delta > 0$ such that:

$$0 < |x - c| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$$
  • Asymmetric Rule: Compute distances $\delta_- = c - x_{\text{left}}$ and $\delta_+ = x_{\text{right}} - c$, then pick $\delta = \min(\delta_-, \delta_+)$.

Chapter 2 · Sections 2.5 – 2.6 · Continuity & Asymptotes

The 3-Part Continuity Test & Intermediate Value Theorem

  • Continuity Checklist at $x = c$: (1) $f(c)$ is defined, (2) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists ($L^- = L^+$), and (3) $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.
  • 3 Discontinuity Types: Removable (hole where limit exists), Jump ($L^- \ne L^+$), and Infinite (vertical asymptote $x = c$).
  • Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT): If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $f(a)\cdot f(b) < 0$, then $f(c) = 0$ for at least one root $c \in (a,b)$.
$$\text{Continuous on } [a,b] \implies \text{No jumps, holes, or vertical asymptotes}$$

Chapter 3 · Sections 3.1 – 3.2 · Interactive Derivative Lab

Secant-to-Tangent Convergence & Differentiability

$$f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0+h) - f(x_0)}{h}$$
  • Differentiable $\implies$ Continuous: Always true! Converse is false ($|x|$ at $0$ is continuous but has a sharp corner).
  • 3 Failure Modes: Corners ($|x|$), Cusps ($x^{2/3}$), Vertical Tangents ($x^{1/3}$), or any Discontinuity.

Chapter 3 · Sections 3.3 – 3.8 · Differentiation Engine

Product, Quotient, Chain Rule & Implicit Differentiation

1. Product & Quotient

Never Multiply Derivatives!

$(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ and $\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{v u' - u v'}{v^2}$ (Low d-High minus High d-Low).

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\tan x] = \sec^2 x$$
2. The Chain Rule

Outside-In Peeling

Differentiate the outer shell evaluated at the untouched inner core $g(x)$, then multiply by $g'(x)$.

$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$$
3. Implicit & Related Rates

Treat $y = y(x)$

Every $y$-term differentiated with respect to $x$ picks up a $\frac{dy}{dx}$ factor. Substitute numbers only after differentiating!

$$\frac{d}{dx}[y^n] = n y^{n-1} \frac{dy}{dx}$$

Chapter 4 · Sections 4.1 – 4.4 · Analytical Applications

Extreme Value Theorem, Mean Value Theorem & Curve Sketching

  • Closed Interval Method (§4.1): Evaluate $f$ at critical points ($f'(c)=0$ or undefined) AND endpoints $a, b$.
  • Mean Value Theorem (§4.2): If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then $\exists c \in (a,b)$ where instantaneous slope equals average secant slope.
  • Concavity & Inflection (§4.4): $f''(x) > 0$ is concave up; inflection points require $f''$ to actually change sign ($y=x^4$ at $0$ is NOT an inflection point).
$$f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$

Chapters 3.9 & 4.5–4.7 · Approximation & Exam Readiness

Linearization, L'Hôpital's Rule & Approximation Accuracy

  • Linearization (§3.9): $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$ with differential $dy = f'(a)\,dx$. Concave down ($f'' < 0$) guarantees $L(x)$ is an overestimate.
  • L'Hôpital's Rule (§4.5): Verify $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ FIRST, then differentiate numerator and denominator separately ($\lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$, NOT Quotient Rule!).
  • Newton's Method (§4.7): $x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$ doubles accurate digits per step.
$$L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a), \qquad x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$$
